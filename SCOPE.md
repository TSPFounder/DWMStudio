# SCOPE.md — Dream World Maker MVP

**Target:** Playable vertical slice by **September 1, 2026**
**Engine:** Unreal Engine 5.3 (both DWM and DWM_Dev for the MVP)
**Rule:** The date is fixed. Fidelity flexes. Nothing moves from *Out* to *In* without a dated entry in the Decisions Log below, stating what is being cut to make room.

---

## Definition of Done

The MVP is shipped when a person can, without developer intervention:

1. Load the DWM world in UE 5.3 and move an avatar through five distinct communities (Mountain, Hillside, Valley, Suburb, City).
2. See each community's headline resources and current Stone (St) balance on screen, sourced from the authoritative SQLite ledger.
3. Execute at least one cross-community trade that mints Stone correctly (buyer negative, seller positive, network total = 0).
4. Watch a Dollar Vault deplete and a Cascading Failure warning state trigger when a vault is exhausted.
5. Observe one engineered system promoted through DWM_Dev → verification gate → DWM (the Mountain wind-turbine, driven for the MVP by the **R2011a Simulink turbine model** — see the 2026-08-02 entry; the Simscape-physics option is now a post-MVP upgrade and the analytic fallback below is no longer needed).

## CONTINGENCY: Turbine Data Source Is Decoupled From the MVP Launch Date

> **⚑ RESOLVED 2026-08-02 — this contingency was never needed and is now
> retained for its reasoning, not its plan.** A working **Simulink turbine
> model runs on the existing R2011a licence**, and the blade geometry is
> built in Fusion. The MVP ships on real engineering-model output, not on
> the analytic fallback described below, and **no MathWorks purchase is
> required for the MVP at all.** The section is kept because the decoupling
> argument still holds and the honesty clause at the end applies MORE
> forcefully now, not less — see the 2026-08-02 Decisions Log entry for what
> the R2011a model is and, importantly, what it is not.

**Decision (2026-07-02): the Sept 1 launch does NOT wait on MathWorks/Simscape licensing being
resolved.** MATLAB/Simscape acquisition is tracked as its own item (see Decisions Log) with its
own timeline; it is explicitly NOT allowed to become a blocker on the MVP ship date.

**Why this works with zero architecture change:** `WorldPackageExporter.cs` already has exactly
the mechanism needed — `WritePendulum`'s `simResultsCsv` parameter falls back to
`GenerateSmallAngleFallback()` (an analytic motion curve) when no real Simscape-exported CSV
exists. The UE-side reader (`DwmGameInstance`/`ADwmPendulumActor`) reads `SimSamples` from the
package and has no idea, and no need to know, whether the data came from Simscape or an
analytic/hand-tuned curve. **Same pattern applies to the turbine mechanism.**

**The plan:**
- **If Simscape Multibody is licensed and the real CAD-linked turbine model is verified through
  the gate before Week 7 — ship with real Simscape physics data**, exactly as originally planned.
- **If not — ship Week 7 with the turbine driven by a hand-tuned or analytic motion curve**
  (same fallback mechanism, extended from the pendulum to the turbine's actual motion profile).
  The Verification Gate ceremony and ledger/economy behavior are IDENTICAL either way; only the
  data source behind `SimSamples` differs. Definition-of-Done item 5 above is satisfied by either.
- **Swapping in real Simscape data later is a fast-follow, not a re-launch.** Once Simscape is
  licensed, re-run the real pipeline, export the real CSV, and replace the fallback data in a
  routine content update — no UE code changes, no re-architecture, no schedule risk to anything
  already shipped.
- **Be honest in the campaign/demo materials about which data source is live** at time of
  recording — don't imply real CAD-verified physics if shipping on the fallback curve. The
  engineering-rigor thesis is still demonstrated by the PIPELINE and the GATE (the promotion
  ceremony, the verification discipline) even when the underlying motion data is provisional.

| 2026-08-01 | RECORDED: real capability limits of the physical manufacturing kit, so the post-MVP arc is grounded in what the hardware actually does rather than what the marketing implies. LASER: Creality Falcon2 40W (diode, 8x5.5W combined) in a Creality Laser Engraver Enclosure 2.0 (28.3 x 28.3 x 15.7 in, vented/fireproof). Manufacturer spec is 0.1-0.15 mm stainless in one pass -- that is SHIM AND FOIL, not sheet: a drink-can wall is ~0.10 mm, thin bracket stock is 0.45 mm, typical brake-formed parts are 1.5 mm. It CANNOT cut usable sheet metal. What it does well is 30 mm acrylic, 20 mm cork, plywood and MDF -- so its real role is jigs, fixtures, templates, gaskets, stencils, and FOUNDRY PATTERNS AND CORE BOXES, sitting beside the 3D-printed-pattern idea. KEY WORKAROUND: laser MARKING is a different energy budget from cutting and is thickness-independent, so the laser can scribe cut lines, bend lines, hole centres and part numbers onto sheet of ANY gauge, which is then cut manually. That requires a THROATLESS (Beverly) SHEAR or nibbler for curves -- a guillotine or bench shear cuts straight lines only. Realistic hand-cut-to-scribed-line accuracy is +/-0.5 mm. General rule: CO2 lasers do NOT cut bare metal (10.6 um is highly reflective); fiber lasers do; diodes mark it. If the fiction wants metal profile cutting, that is a fiber laser or a CNC plasma table, and the choice bounds what the tool can plausibly make in-story. ALUMINIUM (added same day): the laser CANNOT cut it, and aluminium is a worse case than steel -- bare aluminium is highly reflective at 455 nm, and more importantly a polished surface at normal incidence reflects a large fraction of the beam back INTO the laser module. Diode arrays have no optical isolator (fiber lasers do), so sustained back-reflection destroys the diodes and focusing optics with no obvious warning. What DOES work on aluminium: (a) marking ANODISED aluminium is excellent and is genuine material removal -- the laser ablates the dye and anodic layer, 5-25 um deep, crisp bright-on-dark, low power so back-reflection is a non-issue; (b) marking BARE aluminium needs a compound (CerMark / moly spray), which fuses TO the surface and is therefore an additive bonded layer, not a recess; (c) for REAL DEPTH in bare aluminium, use the laser as a RESIST MASK -- coat the stock (paint, vinyl, toner), laser-ablate the pattern through the coating (burning paint, not metal, so reflectivity is irrelevant), chemically etch the exposed metal with sodium hydroxide, then strip the resist. That route gets true etched depth into bare aluminium with the machine already owned. HIGH-VALUE APPLICATION: laser-etched graduations on anodised aluminium dials and scales, which is exactly what the Gingery dividing head (book 5) needs and is far easier than cutting divisions mechanically -- likewise machine nameplates and control-panel legends. | -- | Two safety items that belong with the kit, not buried in chat: never laser PVC or vinyl (chlorine gas, and it corrodes the machine), and never laser galvanised or coated steel even for marking (zinc fumes) -- the enclosure's extraction reduces exposure, it does not eliminate it. MDF machining dust is a genuine respiratory hazard requiring extraction and a proper respirator, not a nuisance mask. ENGINEERING NOTE: a scribed line is a stress raiser, so do not scribe across the tension surface of any cyclically-loaded part -- same fatigue reasoning that drives the material selections in Wind_Turbine_BOM.xlsx. TWO FURTHER HAZARDS from the aluminium note: back-reflection off polished metal can destroy the laser module silently, so never point it at bare reflective stock at cutting power -- scuff to matte or use marking compound, which kills the reflectivity as well as giving the mark. And the resist-mask etching route uses sodium hydroxide on aluminium, which EVOLVES HYDROGEN GAS -- flammable and it accumulates. Ventilate, no ignition sources, eye protection mandatory with caustic. |
